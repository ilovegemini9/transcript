How to Transcribe a Video in Another Language: Tips for Better Results

Learn what affects multilingual video transcription and how to review dialects, mixed speech, names, and translations.

Quick answer: Start with an accessible public video, generate its transcript, and verify names, numbers, and important wording against the original before you rely on the text.

Language detection is not the same as translation

Transcription aims to write down the words spoken in the source language. Translation converts meaning into another language. A transcript in Arabic, Spanish, French, or another language should not automatically be assumed to be an English translation.

If you need both, create and review the source-language transcript first, then translate it as a separate step. Keeping the versions distinct makes it easier to check whether the translation preserves the original meaning.

Accents, dialects, and mixed-language speech

Regional pronunciation, local vocabulary, code-switching, and borrowed words can challenge recognition. A speaker may move between languages or use a brand name that does not follow the pronunciation rules of either language.

Check names and phrases that look unusually standardized. If a provider supports language selection, choose the language that best matches the source. If the video changes language, review those sections manually.

Review multilingual transcripts carefully

Ask a fluent reader to check idioms, names, and phrases whose meaning depends on local context. Verify numbers, dates, and negations, and keep the original audio available for disputed passages.

Do not mix translated text into the original transcript without labeling it. Use separate headings or files for the source transcript and translated version, and record which language each represents.

Choose a tool with realistic expectations

Language availability and accuracy vary by provider and model. A service may support many languages but perform differently across dialects, audio conditions, and code-switching patterns.

For important interviews, legal material, medical information, or public statements, use a qualified human transcriber or reviewer. Automated tools can assist, but should not be treated as a guarantee of exact wording.

Frequently asked questions

Does transcription translate the video?

No. Transcription writes the speech in text; translation is a separate process.

Can a transcript include two languages?

It can when the recognition system handles code-switching, but mixed-language sections should be reviewed.

Are all dialects equally accurate?

No. Performance varies by provider, language, dialect, recording quality, and vocabulary.
